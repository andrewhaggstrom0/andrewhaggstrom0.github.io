F1TENTH Autonomous Racing

Robotics Follow the Gap RRT Pure Pursuit MPPI LiDAR

A Carnegie Mellon course project on a 1/10-scale autonomous race car. I implemented three approaches to the same driving problem, in increasing order of sophistication: a reactive gap follower, a sampling-based planner paired with a path tracker, and a sampling-based model-predictive controller.

Follow the Gap

A purely reactive method that works directly from the LiDAR scan. It preprocesses the scan, places a safety bubble around the nearest obstacle, then steers toward the largest remaining free gap.

RRT + Pure Pursuit

RRT grows a tree of collision-free samples to plan a local path around obstacles. Pure Pursuit then tracks that path geometrically, steering the car toward a lookahead point along it.

MPPI

Model Predictive Path Integral control is a sampling-based form of model-predictive control. It rolls out many perturbed control sequences through a vehicle model, scores each one with a cost function, and applies the cost-weighted average of the sequences.

Hardware & VESC Tuning

Good controllers only help if the car can execute their commands, so I also worked at the hardware level. Understanding how the motor and drivetrain respond let me tune the VESC motor controller to keep the car's accelerations very smooth.
