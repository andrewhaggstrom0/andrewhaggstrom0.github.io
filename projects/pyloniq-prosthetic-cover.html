PylonIQ Prosthetic Cover

Biomedical Hardware Android

Overview

A smart prosthetic pylon cover designed for below-the-knee amputees that combines aesthetic customization with embedded gait analysis and maintenance monitoring. Built as a capstone project in partnership with Phoenix Biomedical, the device addresses the 11–22% prosthetic abandonment rate driven by aesthetic dissatisfaction and lack of maintenance awareness. The design delivers a 3D-printed, patient-customizable cover with onboard IMU sensing and an Android companion app. This project was selected as the top capstone project in a department-wide competition among all graduating Biomedical Engineering seniors.

Key Features

  • Custom 3D-printed enclosure: The pylon cover is manufactured from PLA via FDM 3D printing, available in 110 colors and scalable in CAD to fit adult prosthetic pylon lengths from the 5th to 95th height percentile; the two-piece design uses embedded magnets for tool-free attachment and detachment averaging under 3 seconds, weighs 263g, withstands horizontal impacts up to 1,493N, and carries an IPX4 waterproof rating.
  • Arduino Nano RP2040 with IMU-based gait analysis: An IMU sampling at 42 Hz collects 3-axis accelerometer and gyroscope data; firmware written in C++ on the Arduino IDE establishes a user-specific gait baseline on first use and continuously flags deviations, with data transmitted over Bluetooth to the companion app at an average latency of 29ms; a rechargeable lithium-ion battery pack powers the system for over 15 hours on a single charge, recharging fully in 5.5 hours.
  • Android companion app in Kotlin: Built natively in Kotlin for Android, the app pairs with the Arduino over Bluetooth, displays daily walking reports, and issues alerts for irregular gait or predicted maintenance needs; data is stored locally and exportable as CSV for gait analysis; UI was designed to WCAG-aligned text size standards and validated for usability across a 20-person focus group.

Results

The prototype passed 14 of 15 verification tests, with 100% Bluetooth data completeness, sub-30ms transmission latency, and a total BOM cost of $35.87. Mechanical validation with 28 participants yielded 100% reporting the device was very easy to detach and over 90% rating attachment as very easy. Phoenix Biomedical validated that the device meets all stated mechanical, electrical, and aesthetic requirements, with IRB approval pending for clinical trials with prosthetic users.
